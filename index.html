Why this matters — the case, stop by stop

TOD was notified in April 2026

A forward-looking decision to integrate settlements with Metro infrastructure — then apparently withdrawn from these areas only months later under MPD-2047.

Nothing on the ground has changed

Congestion hasn't reduced, infrastructure gaps remain, and the need for Metro connectivity hasn't diminished — if anything, it's more urgent.

Villages already face severe congestion

Residents travel long distances for basic needs, deepening dependence on private vehicles on already strained roads.

Civic facilities are inadequate

Schools, healthcare, fire stations and neighbourhood shopping are all in short supply — TOD lets these be planned in, not left out.

This is about quality of life

Not a request for extra FAR or development rights — the goal is walkable access to shops, schools, healthcare and the Metro itself.

Less driving, cleaner air

Bringing homes and services closer to transit is part of the answer to Delhi's congestion and pollution, not separate from it.

Public investment deserves full use

Metro infrastructure only pays off if the areas around it are planned so residents can actually rely on it day to day.

Why deny it to those who need it most?

The villages facing exactly the problems TOD is meant to solve should be assessed on their actual ground conditions — not excluded by default.

Planned, not uncontrolled

The ask is for regulated, infrastructure-led development — with safeguards on road width, parking, fire safety and public services.

Six direct questions for the authorities

Why were these areas fit for TOD in April? What changed by MPD-2047? Under which provision were they excluded, and on what assessment?

The collective request

Retain or restore TOD for appropriate LDRA villages along the Metro corridor, in the interest of thousands of residents — not individual landowners.
